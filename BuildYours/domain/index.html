Bonus

Get yourname.com

A free address like yourname.github.io works. Your own name as the address looks more finished on a résumé, and it stays yours if you ever change hosts. I recommend it.

Is your name available?

Type your name and I'll suggest addresses, then check them with the registry for .com and .net.

    Checking sends only the domain name to Verisign, the registry for .com and .net, and only when you click Check. A result is a snapshot, not a hold, and Route 53's own search has the final say: a few names are reserved or carry premium prices.

    What it costs

    A .com domain at Amazon Route 53about $16 a year* Route 53 DNS for your domain$0.50 a month* Total for the first yearabout $22*

    *Prices as of November 2026, before taxes. Other endings, such as .dev, cost a different amount. Check Route 53 pricing before you buy.

    Where is your site hosted?

    The last part of the setup depends on it.

    Set it up

    1. 1
      In AWS
      Create an AWS account

      Sign up at aws.amazon.com. It asks for a payment card, because the domain is a paid purchase.

    2. 2
      In Route 53
      Search for your name and register it
      1. Open the Route 53 console and choose Registered domains, then Register domains.
      2. Search for the name you want, such as alexkim.com. If it's taken, try your middle initial or alexkim.dev.
      3. Choose it, keep the length at one year, and leave auto-renew on so you don't lose it.
      4. Enter your contact details. Leave privacy protection on, so your address isn't public.
      5. Review and submit.
    3. 3
      In your email
      Confirm your email address

      You'll get an email asking you to verify your contact address. Click the link. If you skip it, the domain can be suspended.

      Registration usually finishes within an hour, but it can take longer. Route 53 creates a hosted zone for the domain automatically; that's where your DNS records go.

    4. 4
      In GitHub
      Add the domain to your site first

      In your site's repository, open Settings, then Pages. Under Custom domain, type alexkim.com (your domain) and click Save.

      Do this before adding the DNS records. GitHub warns that records pointing at Pages without the domain added in your repository can let someone else claim your address.

    5. 5
      In Route 53
      Point your domain at GitHub Pages

      Open Hosted zones, choose your domain, and click Create record. Make these two records:

      Record 1: your bare domain
      Name
      Leave blank
      Type
      A
      Value
      185.199.108.153
      185.199.109.153
      185.199.110.153
      185.199.111.153
      (one per line)
      Record 2: the www version
      Name
      www
      Type
      CNAME
      Value
      your-username.github.io, using your GitHub username

      Addresses from GitHub's guide, Managing a custom domain for your GitHub Pages site.

    6. 6
      In GitHub
      Turn on HTTPS

      Back in Settings → Pages, wait for the DNS check to pass, then tick Enforce HTTPS. It can take a little while for the option to become available.

    7. 4
      In Vercel
      Add the domain to your project

      Open your project, go to Settings, then Domains, and add alexkim.com (your domain). Add the www version too if Vercel offers it. Vercel then shows the exact DNS values for your project on the domain card.

    8. 5
      In Route 53
      Point your domain at Vercel

      Open Hosted zones, choose your domain, and click Create record. Copy the values from your Vercel domain card:

      Record 1: your bare domain
      Name
      Leave blank
      Type
      A
      Value
      The IP address on your domain card, usually 76.76.21.21
      Record 2: the www version
      Name
      www
      Type
      CNAME
      Value
      The CNAME value on your domain card

      From Vercel's guide, Can I use my domain on Vercel with A records? Delete any AAAA record for the bare domain if one exists.

    9. 6
      In Vercel
      Wait for the check to turn green

      Vercel checks the records and sets up HTTPS on its own once they're found.

    10. 7
      In your browser
      Open your new address

      Try both alexkim.com and www.alexkim.com. DNS changes usually show up within minutes, but can take up to a day or two. Then update your résumé and LinkedIn with the new address.

    Under the hood · DNS

    DNS is the internet's address book. Your A record says "this name lives at these IP addresses." Your CNAME says "www is another name for that host." Registering the domain makes you its owner; the records tell browsers where to find your site.
