The rubric I train to

What I look for in engineers in the AI age

This is the rubric I wrote for an associate developer's first six months. It is what I train to, coach against and evaluate with: twenty criteria, each with a plain description of what meets the bar, what exceeds it and what falls short, and the check-in where it should first be met.

The short version

Four things I expect from anyone who builds with AI

The first four criteria are about AI itself. Using it is expected by month two. Understanding and owning what it produced is expected by month four.

Use it

AI is the default way to plan, build, test, review and document work.

By month 2
Direct it

Break the problem down and give the tool the context, requirements and standards it needs.

By month 2
Understand it

Explain how the solution works, and why, without the AI's help.

By month 4
Verify it

Review, test and correct generated work. Own the quality of everything submitted.

By month 4

The other sixteen cover what AI does not do for you: standing up and securing real systems, and working well with the people around you.

Engineering and Development

10 criteria

AI across the whole lifecycle, and the fundamentals underneath it.

01

Builds software using AI tools across the development lifecycle

Month 2

Meets

Uses AI tools as the default way to plan, build, test, review, and document work

Exceeds

Finds new ways to apply AI across the lifecycle and shares workflows that make the team faster

Does not meet

Avoids AI tools or uses them only occasionally, slowing delivery

02

Directs AI effectively with clear context, requirements, and constraints

Month 2

Meets

Breaks problems down and gives AI tools the product context, requirements, and standards needed to get a usable result

Exceeds

Produces accurate results in few iterations and builds reusable prompts, rules, or context files for the team

Does not meet

Gives vague instructions and accepts whatever comes back, or cannot break a problem into workable pieces

03

Understands what is under the hood of AI-built code and why it is built that way

Month 4

Meets

Can explain how the solution works, the design choices behind it, and the trade-offs, without the AI's help

Exceeds

Challenges and improves AI-proposed designs, choosing simpler or more suitable approaches with clear reasoning

Does not meet

Cannot explain code they submitted or why an approach was chosen

04

Verifies AI output before it ships

Month 4

Meets

Reviews, tests, and corrects AI-generated work; owns the quality of everything submitted

Exceeds

Catches subtle errors and edge cases, and strengthens automated tests and checks that guard AI-built changes

Does not meet

Submits generated work unreviewed or untested; errors are caught by others

05

Sets up applications end to end

Month 4

Meets

Can stand up a new application: repository, local environment, configuration, pipeline, and deployment through each environment

Exceeds

Sets up applications confidently with minimal guidance and improves templates or setup docs for the next person

Does not meet

Cannot get an application running or deployed without someone else doing the setup

06

Understands Terraform and infrastructure as code

Month 4

Meets

Reads, explains, and makes routine changes to Terraform; understands plan, apply, and state

Exceeds

Writes clean, reusable Terraform and anticipates the impact of a change before applying it

Does not meet

Cannot interpret Terraform, or applies changes without understanding their effect

07

Understands AWS and the core services the team uses

Month 6

Meets

Explains what each core service does (compute, storage, databases, networking, IAM) and how the application uses them

Exceeds

Chooses appropriate services for a problem with cost, scale, and reliability in mind, and troubleshoots cloud issues independently

Does not meet

Treats cloud infrastructure as a black box or cannot find and inspect resources

08

Understands authentication and authorization

Month 6

Meets

Explains how users and services prove identity and receive permissions (tokens, sessions, roles) and implements them using team standards

Exceeds

Spots auth weaknesses in designs or AI-generated code and proposes sound fixes

Does not meet

Confuses authentication with authorization or introduces access-control gaps

09

Understands security fundamentals

Month 4

Meets

Handles secrets and sensitive data correctly, applies least privilege, keeps dependencies current, and knows what must not be shared with AI tools

Exceeds

Identifies security risks early in their own and others' work and raises them constructively

Does not meet

Exposes secrets or data, grants broad permissions, or ignores security guidance

10

Understands how systems talk to each other

Month 6

Meets

Can trace a request across services and explain the APIs, networking, events, and data flows involved

Exceeds

Diagnoses cross-system issues using logs and metrics, and diagrams integrations clearly for others

Does not meet

Cannot describe how the application connects to other systems or where to look when an integration fails

Collaboration and Knowledge Sharing

3 criteria

Work that other people can find, follow and build on.

11

Creates clear, easily accessible documentation for the team

Month 4

Meets

Documentation is well written, current, and easy for others to find

Exceeds

Creates documentation that others rely on for onboarding, handoffs, or reference

Does not meet

Documentation is missing, unclear, or disorganized

12

Listens to review feedback and gives feedback when asked

Month 2

Meets

Applies feedback meaningfully and offers support when asked

Exceeds

Actively applies feedback and begins offering thoughtful, constructive feedback to peers

Does not meet

Ignores or resists feedback, or avoids giving feedback when asked

13

Works effectively with teammates and partners in other roles

Month 6

Meets

Pairs willingly, shares work in progress, and collaborates well with design, product, and QA

Exceeds

Strengthens the team by volunteering help, sharing what they learn, and building trust across roles

Does not meet

Works in isolation or is difficult to collaborate with

Communication

4 criteria

Listening, writing, and asking for help at the right moment.

14

Actively listens in meetings, takes notes, and shares them with the team

Month 2

Meets

Listens carefully, captures relevant information, and shares organized notes proactively

Exceeds

Comes prepared, asks insightful questions, tracks follow-ups independently, and shares notes that keep the team aligned

Does not meet

Misses key points, or notes are disorganized or not shared when needed

15

Communicates effectively and respectfully in conversation and in writing

Month 4

Meets

Communicates clearly and respectfully, balances listening with talking, and writes clear updates and messages

Exceeds

Communicates clearly and confidently across audiences while maintaining a strong balance of listening

Does not meet

Dominates conversations, goes silent, or communicates in ways others struggle to follow

16

Asks for guidance when necessary or blocked

Month 2

Meets

Seeks help appropriately, after a reasonable attempt, to avoid staying blocked

Exceeds

Anticipates blockers and seeks guidance early to maintain progress

Does not meet

Remains stuck without asking for help, or asks before making any attempt

17

Understands tasks and their acceptance criteria and goals

Month 2

Meets

Demonstrates clear understanding of assigned tasks before starting work

Exceeds

Clarifies ambiguous requirements in ways that benefit the broader team

Does not meet

Lacks clarity on task requirements or goals

Above the Line Behavior

3 criteria

How someone shows up when the work gets hard.

18

Authentic Communication

Month 6

Meets

Listens well, confirms understanding, avoids ambiguity

Exceeds

Demonstrates strong self-awareness and clear intent in all communication

Does not meet

Interrupts, assumes, or communicates vaguely

19

Constructive Negotiation

Month 6

Meets

Collaborates respectfully and moves work forward

Exceeds

Helps resolve minor disagreements constructively and respectfully

Does not meet

Resists alignment, is combative or passive

20

Impeccable Coordination

Month 6

Meets

Keeps stakeholders informed and aligned

Exceeds

Keeps relevant teammates informed proactively without being prompted

Does not meet

Leaves others out of the loop or miscommunicates progress
